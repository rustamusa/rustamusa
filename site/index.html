What became possible with AI

The work happens inside the tools a business already has. ChatGPT, Claude and Grok, picked to fit the job.

The inbox, checked all day

New requests are found in Outlook, replies are drafted with the quote attached, and a daily digest lists every new lead.

The website and the store, from the inside

In WordPress and the store admin, updates go out in batches with a backup first. Buy buttons, redirects and a phone bar are added without a rebuild.

Drawings from one 3D model

Plans, details, weights and renders come from the same model, with a file AutoCAD opens at full scale. A change is made once and the whole set follows.

A link the client opens the same day

A builder, a website or a 3D walk-through goes onto a private link as soon as it runs. The client looks, asks for a change and gets the next version.

Proposals and reports as web pages

A quote goes out as a link: options side by side, drawings, one total. The owner gets a report of the work as one page.

An assistant on the website

It answers prices at night and on weekends, takes a name and a phone, and emails the lead to staff. Three business websites have one now.

All of it in parallel

Email, estimates, drawings, website fixes and posts move at the same time, for four businesses, with one person checking each result.

How AI is kept in check

The owner says yes first

Nothing goes live without the owner’s OK. Of twelve website improvements proposed for the game venues, the owner chose nine and turned down three.

A backup before every change

Each change to a live website starts with a full backup and ends with a check of the live pages.

A person reads it before it is sent

AI drafts emails, quotes and drawings. A person reads each one, and a drawing set is checked by the cab maker and issued for approval before anything is built.

The assistant only says what the website says

It answers from the site’s own pages and prices, never guesses, and hands the customer to staff. It will not change its rules for a stranger.

Private things stay private

Client names, addresses and prices are removed from every picture here. A client’s 3D walk-through stays on a private link.

The team learns to run it

The work is done at the business, with the owner or staff at the keyboard. The goal each time: by evening the team does it without help.

  1. Morning

    Watch the flow

    How does a call, an email or a walk-in turn into money? One real order followed start to finish. Every stall written down.

  2. Midday

    The owner picks one thing

    The findings go on the table. The owner chooses what to fix first.

  3. Afternoon

    Built together

    On the business's own accounts, with real examples, the owner or staff at the keyboard.

  4. End of day

    The team runs it

    Staff do it themselves, watched once. A one-page how-to stays behind. After that, help is one text away.

One rule

Only what moves money or saves time gets touched: the quote, the order, the booking, the reply. The rest of the business stays as built.

What stays with the owner

A report in plain English

Every few weeks the owner gets one page: what was built, what was fixed, what comes next. Five have gone out since September.

The owner sets the rules

Before the Cyber Monday 2026 tickets were set up, the owner answered nine questions about how they should work. Each answer became a setting.

The owner tests it himself

The first test purchase of a Cyber Monday ticket was made by the owner, with a real card.

Taught by someone who learned it the same way

Not a programmer: a sales manager who learned to build with AI on real jobs. Outside work, a personal trainer who coaches people one on one.
